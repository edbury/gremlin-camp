# The Great Wood

> Stonetop sits on a bluff overlooking the Great Wood, which stretches hundreds of miles to the east. Few villagers dare to even cross the Stream that flows near the base of the bluff. Hunters, trappers, and foragers go deeper, but even they venture a day or two into the forest at most.
>
> The terrain is hilly and rugged, laced with small streams and dotted with ponds. The trees are a mix of deciduous and evergreen. In some spots, the trees grow tightly, surrounded by thick brush so that one can barely walk. In others, massive old growth trees dominate, the space between them sparse and open.

> A few brave souls ply its depths for meat and fur to trade with Stonetop's neighbors. Trappers bring in most of the wealth, but hunters get more respect. It's dangerous down there.
>
> By compact with the Forest Folk, the villagers never cut down a living tree. They're free to gather fallen wood, but if they need timber they send folk northwest to the Foothills. Yet no one's laid eyes on the Folk for a decade, and some grumble that there's plenty of timber down there, in their own back yard.
>
> The crinwin—diminutive, gray-skinned creatures with a penchant for mimicry—lurk in the Great Wood. The Forest Folk once hunted them and kept them in check, but their numbers have been growing.
>
> The Wood is also home to the strange and magical Fae. The Forest Folk called them "Good Neighbors" and took pains to avoid insulting them. They cautioned Stonetop to do the same.
